# Attack Vectors — Where Real Estate / PE Deals Are Thin

Use this to find the weakest assumption fast. Every line is a place underwriters talk
themselves into a number. Always re-express rent as **$/SF/yr** before comparing.

## Cross-Cutting (check on every deal first)

These move returns the most and are attacked first by any serious LP or IC.

- **Exit cap vs. going-in cap.** If exit cap ≤ going-in cap, the model is underwriting
  cap-rate compression. That is a bet on the rate environment, not on the asset. Demand
  exit cap ≥ going-in + 25–50 bps unless there's a hard story (lease-up to stabilized,
  vintage repricing). A 25 bps move on exit cap can swing IRR by several hundred bps.
- **The number talked into.** Find the input that, if nudged, flips the deal below the
  hurdle. It's usually exit cap, terminal rent growth, or refinance proceeds. If the base
  case sits exactly on a round-number hurdle (e.g. 15.0% IRR, 2.0x), it was reverse-
  engineered. Say so.
- **Residual / reversion as % of total profit.** If >50% of the profit (or PV of cash
  flows) comes from the terminal sale, this is a bet on exit, not on operations. Hostile
  readers attack this first because it's the softest dollar in the model.
- **Rent growth curve.** Flat 3% for ten years is a fantasy in most markets. Check it
  against trailing 5–10 yr CPI/market rent CAGR. Front-loaded growth that fades is more
  defensible than a constant. Compounding 3% vs 2% over a 7-yr hold is a large terminal-
  value difference.
- **Going-in basis vs. replacement cost and comps.** $/SF (or $/unit, $/key) versus
  recent trades and cost to build. Buying above replacement cost needs a reason. Buying
  at a record-low cap with no comp support is the first re-trade lever.
- **Leverage and the refi.** Does the model assume a refinance that pulls out equity at
  a cap rate / DSCR the market won't underwrite at exit? Check refi proceeds against a
  stressed take-out coupon and a lender's actual DSCR/debt-yield minimums.
- **DSCR / debt yield through the hold.** Where is the trough? If DSCR dips below ~1.20x
  (or debt yield below lender minimums) in any year, the deal has a covenant/cash-trap
  problem the model may be papering over.
- **Opex ratio and the "we'll cut costs" line.** Underwritten opex per SF/unit below the
  trailing actuals is a classic talked-into number. Taxes especially: many models hold
  taxes flat or use seller's assessed value, ignoring reassessment on sale.
- **Promote / waterfall optics.** Make sure the IRR shown is the LP's net-of-fees-and-
  promote return, not the gross deal IRR. The spread between them is where LPs feel
  misled.

## Office

- **Mark-to-market on rollover.** In-place rents vs. current asking in **$/SF/yr** —
  but net of concessions. A "$45/SF face" with 12 months free on a 7-yr term is closer
  to ~$38/SF/yr net effective. If the model rolls expiring leases to face rents, it's
  overstating cash flow.
- **TI/LC per SF.** New office leasing costs $80–150+/SF in TI+LC. Models that lease up
  vacancy with thin leasing capital are hiding the real cost of NOI.
- **Downtime / absorption.** Months of vacancy between leases. Aggressive re-lease in
  6 months in a soft submarket is a tell.
- **Credit and WALT.** Weighted average lease term and tenant credit. Short WALT + roll
  into a weak submarket = the cash flow is a mirage at exit.
- **Submarket vacancy reality.** Underwritten stabilized occupancy vs. actual submarket
  vacancy. 92% stabilized in a 20%-vacant submarket needs proof.

## Multifamily

- **Loss-to-lease and the renovation premium.** The "we'll push rents $X/unit on
  turn" — convert to **$/SF/yr** and compare to true renovated comps, not asking rents.
  Check the assumed renovation pace (units/month) against staffing reality.
- **Trended rents vs. in-place.** Year-1 underwritten rent already above in-place is a
  talked-into number unless there's a real loss-to-lease gap.
- **Other income / RUBS / fees.** Aggressive utility reimbursement and fee income that
  outpaces actuals.
- **Real estate taxes on sale.** Reassessment at the new (higher) basis is the single
  most common multifamily miss. Hold-flat taxes overstate NOI and exit value.
- **Bad debt / concessions / economic vacancy.** Underwriting 5% economic vacancy in a
  market running 8–10% with concessions.
- **Insurance.** In coastal / wildfire markets, holding insurance flat is no longer
  credible — it's been a double-digit annual grower.

## Industrial / Logistics

- **Mark-to-market magnitude.** Industrial M2M can be huge — but check the in-place vs.
  market gap in **$/SF/yr** against *signed* recent deals, not broker optimism. The risk
  is the gap closing slower or smaller than modeled.
- **Rent growth durability.** Recent double-digit industrial rent growth is not a
  forward assumption. Trending it flat-to-down is more defensible.
- **Clear height / functionality / obsolescence.** Class-B box at Class-A rents.
- **Single-tenant roll risk.** One tenant, lease expiry inside the hold, no renewal
  evidence — binary outcome the model smooths.

## Retail

- **Sales per SF and occupancy cost ratio.** Are tenant sales healthy enough to support
  renewals at modeled rents in **$/SF/yr**? Occupancy cost >10–12% signals roll-down risk.
- **Anchor health and co-tenancy.** Anchor going dark triggers co-tenancy clauses and
  cascading rent relief the model rarely shows.
- **Percentage rent** assumed to grow with no sales support.
- **Capex for redevelopment** understated.

## Hotel

- **RevPAR penetration vs. comp set.** ADR × occupancy assumptions vs. STR comp set;
  penetration index over 100 that keeps climbing is a tell.
- **Flow-through / margin expansion** assumed without a PIP or brand change to justify it.
- **PIP / brand-mandated capex** missing or understated.
- **Cyclicality** — hotels reprice fastest in a downturn; a smooth RevPAR ramp through a
  full hold ignores the cycle.

## Development / Value-Add

- **Hard-cost contingency.** <5% in this environment is a fantasy. Check GMP vs. budget.
- **Construction timeline & interest carry.** Delay adds carry and pushes lease-up into
  a different market. Stress the schedule by 6 months.
- **Lease-up curve / absorption.** Units or SF per month vs. actual market absorption.
  The "stabilize in 12 months" line is the most common dev miss.
- **Exit on unstabilized NOI.** Selling on a forward/stabilized NOI the asset has never
  produced. Demand a trailing-3-month annualized basis.
- **Cost-to-build vs. exit value.** If the trended exit value barely clears cost, the
  development premium has evaporated and the risk isn't priced.

## The "Tell" Patterns (signs a number was reverse-engineered)

- Base-case return sits exactly on the hurdle (15.0%, 2.00x, 7.0% cash yield).
- Exit cap a suspiciously round, low number with no comp support.
- Rent growth or opex growth set to a single flat rate for the whole hold.
- A refinance in the exact year needed to rescue the equity multiple.
- Sensitivity table conspicuously omits the variable that breaks the deal.
- Concessions / free rent stripped out of net effective rent.
- Taxes held at seller's basis through a sale.
