# IC / LP Question Bank — and What Closes Each One

For section 2 (the one question that would make the sponsor sweat) and section 3 (what to
prove). Pick the question with no clean answer given what's actually in the model. Phrase
it the way a tough IC member or a pass-hunting LP would actually say it.

The format of each entry: **Question** → *what closes it.*

## Exit / Reversion

- **"Strip out cap-rate compression — set the exit cap equal to your going-in. What's the
  deal then?"** → Re-rack showing IRR/multiple at exit cap = going-in (and +25/+50 bps).
  If it still clears the hurdle, the deal stands on operations. If it collapses, it's a
  rate bet.
- **"How much of your profit is the sale versus the cash flow you actually collect?"** →
  Split total profit into operating cash vs. reversion. If reversion >50%, defend the
  exit assumptions with broker BOVs and recent comp trades at that cap.
- **"What did the last three comparable assets actually trade at, and at what cap?"** →
  Signed/closed comps (not asking), reconciled to the underwritten exit cap.

## Rent / Revenue

- **"Walk me through your rent assumption in $/SF/yr, net effective — after free rent and
  TIs — versus the last signed lease in this submarket."** → Net-effective comp set with
  concession detail; a leasing broker's signed-deal sheet.
- **"Your loss-to-lease / mark-to-market is $X/SF/yr. Where are the signed deals that
  prove tenants pay that, not the ones brokers hope for?"** → Recent executed leases at
  the target rent; renewal evidence on in-place tenants.
- **"Why does year-one underwritten rent already sit above in-place?"** → Documented
  loss-to-lease, expiring below-market leases, or a renovation premium with renovated
  comps.

## Opex / Taxes

- **"You held taxes flat through a sale. What happens when the assessor reassesses at your
  exit basis?"** → Reassessment analysis at the new basis; comparable reassessment
  outcomes in the jurisdiction; a tax-consultant opinion.
- **"Your opex per SF/unit is below the trailing actuals. What specifically gets cut, and
  who's signed up to deliver it?"** → Line-item operating budget vs. T-12 actuals, with a
  named operational plan, not a haircut.
- **"Insurance flat in this market — really?"** → Bound quote or broker indication; recent
  renewal history.

## Capital Stack / Financing

- **"Your refi pulls $X of equity out in year N. Show me the lender, the coupon, and the
  DSCR/debt-yield they'll actually underwrite at that point."** → Lender term sheet or
  indicative quote; stressed-rate DSCR/debt-yield test.
- **"Where's the DSCR trough across the hold, and how close is it to a cash trap?"** →
  Annual DSCR/debt-yield schedule with covenant thresholds marked.
- **"Is the IRR you're showing the LP net of fees and promote, or the gross deal IRR?"** →
  Net-to-LP waterfall output alongside gross.

## Basis / Underwriting Integrity

- **"You're buying above replacement cost / at a record-low cap. What stops a buyer from
  building new and undercutting you at exit?"** → Cost-to-build analysis; supply pipeline
  data; barriers-to-entry evidence.
- **"Which single input, moved to market, takes this below the hurdle — and why is your
  base case sitting right on it?"** → A sensitivity / tornado chart that *includes* that
  input, and a defense of the base value with third-party data.
- **"What's the downside case — a real recession path, not a 5% haircut — and does the
  equity survive it?"** → A genuine downside scenario (occupancy drop, cap expansion,
  delayed lease-up) with the equity outcome.

## Development / Value-Add

- **"Your contingency is under 5% and the schedule has no slack. What's the GMP, and what's
  the deal if it slips six months into a softer leasing market?"** → GMP contract,
  contingency benchmark, schedule-stress re-rack with added carry.
- **"You're exiting on a stabilized NOI the asset has never produced. Show me trailing-3
  annualized."** → T-3 annualized basis at exit; absorption schedule vs. market actuals.

## How to Use This in the Output

- Section 2 gets **one** question — the sharpest one for this specific deal, not a list.
- Section 3 maps each of the three ranked soft spots to its closer above. Make the proof
  concrete and assignable: "Get a lender term sheet at the year-5 refi" beats "validate
  financing assumptions."
